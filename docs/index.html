Diagnostic Performance of Common Biochemical Screening Tests for Mild Autonomous Cortisol Secretion in Adrenal Incidentalomas: A Systematic Review and Hierarchical SROC Meta-analysis.

Notable
Endocrine practice : official journal of the American College of Endocrinology and the American Association of Clinical Endocrinologists · 2026

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 12 studies (1,420 patients) found that serum dexamethasone suppression testing (DST) had the highest sensitivity (97.9%) and the lowest negative likelihood ratio (0.03) among available screening tests for mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS) in adrenal incidentalomas, supporting its use as a rule-out tool. Salivary DST (sensitivity 74.9%) and late-night salivary cortisol (sensitivity 62.7%) performed more modestly, while urinary free cortisol estimates were too imprecise to interpret reliably. Importantly, substantial threshold heterogeneity and indirect comparisons preclude declaring any single test diagnostically superior, and positive results require clinical contextualization and confirmatory workup.

Caveat: All three serum DST studies used reference definitions that incorporated a dexamethasone-suppression criterion, creating incorporation bias that artificially inflates apparent sensitivity. The sparse study count (n=12) and heterogeneous MACS definitions limit generalizability of the pooled estimates.

A suppressed cortisol after 1-mg overnight DST carries a very low negative likelihood ratio (0.03 in this meta-analysis), making MACS highly unlikely — this result effectively rules out the diagnosis in most clinical contexts. No further biochemical evaluation for autonomous cortisol secretion is warranted in this patient, though routine incidentaloma imaging follow-up per guidelines still applies.

Health-related quality of life and patterns of psychological vulnerability in autoimmune Addison's disease: a systematic review.

Notable
Frontiers in endocrinology · 2026

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review synthesized 23 studies (1,764 patients) on health-related quality of life (HR-QoL) and psychological outcomes specifically in autoimmune primary adrenal insufficiency (PAI). HR-QoL was consistently impaired across studies, most prominently in vitality, energy, and physical functioning domains, with female sex and greater autoimmune comorbidity predicting worse outcomes. Modified-release hydrocortisone and infusion strategies showed more consistent improvements on disease-specific HR-QoL instruments than generic measures, while anxiety and depressive symptoms were generally mild and inconsistently elevated — though mental fatigue and subjective executive dysfunction emerged as more reproducible concerns.

Caveat: Most included studies were observational and cross-sectional, and many used mixed PAI cohorts where autoimmune etiology was predominant but not exclusive. Heterogeneity in outcome instruments limits direct comparison across studies, and interventional trial data remain sparse.
PMID 42851403 · doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1901783
Case: A 34-year-old woman with autoimmune Addison's disease on standard twice-daily hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone reports persistent fatigue and cognitive 'fogginess' despite normal electrolytes and a morning cortisol consistent with adequate replacement. According to this systematic review, what patient-level characteristics are associated with poorer HR-QoL, and what therapeutic modification has shown the most consistent benefit on disease-specific quality-of-life measures?
Female sex and the presence of additional autoimmune comorbidities were repeatedly associated with poorer perceived HR-QoL in this review. Among therapeutic approaches, modified-release hydrocortisone and continuous subcutaneous hydrocortisone infusion strategies demonstrated more consistent improvements on disease-specific HR-QoL instruments compared to standard immediate-release regimens, making these worth considering in patients with persistent impairment despite biochemically adequate replacement.

Surgical management of ectopic Cushing's syndrome from a pulmonary carcinoid secreting CRH supported by intraoperative ACTH monitoring - a case report.

Preliminary
Frontiers in endocrinology · 2026

This case report describes a 36-year-old man with ACTH-dependent hypercortisolism in whom dynamic testing (high-dose DST, metyrapone, desmopressin) yielded inconclusive results that could not reliably distinguish Cushing's disease from ectopic Cushing's syndrome (ECS). A small pulmonary nodule was identified on chest imaging, and given the rapid clinical course, surgical resection was pursued with intraoperative ACTH monitoring; a transient ACTH surge during initial excision prompted extension to upper lobectomy, after which ACTH fell markedly and sustained. Pathology confirmed a typical pulmonary carcinoid, and the patient achieved biochemical remission with gradual HPA axis recovery — illustrating intraoperative ACTH monitoring as a potentially valuable adjunct for surgical decision-making in ambiguous ECS.

Caveat: This is a single case report and cannot support broad practice change. The value of intraoperative ACTH monitoring in ECS remains debated, and the threshold criteria for interpreting intraoperative ACTH changes in this setting are not yet standardized.
