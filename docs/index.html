Selective resection of the cavernous sinus medial wall in functional pituitary adenomas: opening the gate to the cavernous sinus from medial to lateral.

Notable
Journal of neurosurgery · 2026

This single-center retrospective series of 35 patients with hormone-secreting pituitary adenomas (acromegaly, Cushing's disease, TSH-secreting) describes intentional resection of the cavernous sinus medial wall (MWCS) during endoscopic endonasal surgery to access tumor invading the CS. Early endocrine remission was achieved in 91.4% of patients, including those with Knosp grade 2–3 invasion. Complication rates were low—transient DI and hypopituitarism each occurred in ~14%, and there were no ICA injuries or CSF leaks—suggesting that in experienced hands this anatomically targeted approach can extend the reach of curative surgery without a prohibitive safety cost.

Caveat: Small retrospective single-center cohort (n=35) with relatively short and variable follow-up (4–33 months); no control arm for comparison with standard resection. Selection bias is likely given that only surgeons with advanced anatomical expertise performed MWCS resection.
PMID 42962234 · doi.org/10.3171/2026.5.JNS252348
Case: A 38-year-old woman with Cushing's disease has a 1.2 cm ACTH-secreting adenoma with Knosp grade 2 cavernous sinus invasion confirmed on MRI. Her surgeon plans endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal resection. Based on the MWCS resection series, what is the rationale for selectively resecting the cavernous sinus medial wall, and what was the early remission rate reported in this cohort?
Resection of the medial wall of the cavernous sinus removes tumor embedded within that structure and provides direct access to the superior, posterior, and inferior CS compartments, enabling more complete removal of invading tumor without violating the lateral CS. In this 35-patient series, early endocrine remission was achieved in 91.4% of patients across Knosp grades 1–4, with no permanent neurological or vascular complications.

Preoperative HbA1c and postoperative day 1 fasting plasma glucose are associated with early glycemic improvement after transsphenoidal surgery in patients with acromegaly and preoperative dysglycemia: a retrospective cohort study.

Notable
BMC endocrine disorders · 2026

In this retrospective cohort of 175 acromegalic patients with preoperative dysglycemia, early glycemic improvement (~1 month post-transsphenoidal surgery) occurred in 48% overall. Higher preoperative HbA1c independently predicted lower odds of improvement (OR 0.60 per unit), and a postoperative day-1 fasting plasma glucose in the 5.5–7.0 mmol/L range was associated with significantly higher improvement rates (61.9% vs. 35.2%) versus those outside this window. These two readily available markers may help stratify which patients are likely to achieve rapid glycemic normalization after successful surgery.

Caveat: Retrospective, single-center design with ~1-month follow-up as the primary endpoint—durability of glycemic benefit and long-term outcomes are not addressed. Biochemical remission of acromegaly itself was not uniformly confirmed as a covariate, which could confound the glycemic results.
PMID 42963766 · doi.org/10.1186/s12902-026-02459-9
Case: You are counseling a patient with acromegaly and poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 9.8%) prior to transsphenoidal surgery. Based on this study's findings, how should you frame expectations for early postoperative glycemic improvement, and what postoperative day-1 glucose range was associated with the best odds of improvement?
Patients with higher preoperative HbA1c had significantly lower odds of early glycemic improvement (OR 0.60 per unit increase), and improvement rates decreased across HbA1c tertiles from ~70% to ~35%. A postoperative day-1 fasting plasma glucose in the 5.5–7.0 mmol/L range was associated with the highest early improvement rate (61.9%), so this patient should be counseled that early glycemic normalization is less likely given the degree of baseline hyperglycemia, and close postoperative glucose monitoring remains essential.

A case report of osilodrostat treatment in primary bilateral macronodular adrenal hyperplasia associated with bilateral adrenal mass reduction.

Preliminary
Journal of medical case reports · 2026

This case report describes a 60-year-old woman with ARMC5-mutation–confirmed PBMAH who was treated with osilodrostat (11β-hydroxylase inhibitor) after failed adrenal artery embolization. After 23 weeks of therapy, 24-hour urinary free cortisol normalized, and imaging demonstrated significant shrinkage of both adrenal glands. Biochemical control was maintained for 11 months after drug discontinuation, raising the hypothesis that osilodrostat may have disease-modifying effects on adrenal gland size beyond simple cortisol suppression.

Caveat: Single case report; prior adrenal artery embolization (which itself failed to control cortisol) significantly confounds attribution of tumor shrinkage to osilodrostat. No mechanistic explanation for persistent post-treatment remission is established. This observation is hypothesis-generating only and should not alter current PBMAH management protocols.
PMID 42958417 · doi.org/10.1186/s13256-026-06442-9
Case: A 55-year-old woman with ARMC5-confirmed PBMAH is intolerant of surgery and has failed conservative measures. Her endocrinologist considers osilodrostat. Beyond cortisol normalization, what intriguing observation was reported in this case, and why must it be interpreted with significant caution?
Bilateral adrenal mass reduction was observed on imaging during osilodrostat treatment—a novel finding for this drug class in PBMAH. However, prior adrenal artery embolization confounds causal attribution, the observation comes from a single patient, and no prospective or controlled data exist. Until further evidence accumulates, this should be considered a hypothesis-generating signal rather than an established treatment effect.
