Infiltrative and inflammatory systemic disorders involving the pituitary gland.

Notable
Pituitary · 2026

This comprehensive review systematically covers the full spectrum of infiltrative and inflammatory disorders affecting the hypothalamic-pituitary region, including granulomatous, histiocytic, infectious, neoplastic, and autoimmune/hypophysitis etiologies. A key practical framework is offered for neuroimaging interpretation: symmetric stalk/gland enlargement with homogeneous enhancement favors lymphocytic, IgG4-related, or granulomatous hypophysitis, while rim enhancement, necrosis, or bone-destructive changes should prompt consideration of infectious or neoplastic causes. The authors emphasize that arginine vasopressin deficiency with pituitary stalk thickening is highly suggestive of a non-adenomatous lesion, and that a structured diagnostic work-up—incorporating targeted labs, hormonal evaluation, and histopathology when needed—should precede empiric immunosuppression or surgery. Importantly, two parallel management tracks are stressed: urgent corticotroph axis replacement and etiology-specific therapy.

Caveat: This is a narrative review without systematic methodology or meta-analysis; the imaging pattern rules described, while clinically useful, are generalizations with meaningful overlap across etiologies and should not replace multidisciplinary judgment.
PMID 42749874 · doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01758-7
Case: A 45-year-old woman presents with polyuria, fatigue, and headache. MRI shows a thickened pituitary stalk with symmetric, homogeneously enhancing enlargement of the stalk and posterior pituitary. Hormonal evaluation confirms central diabetes insipidus and secondary adrenal insufficiency. What diagnosis should you most strongly consider, and what is the most urgent initial management step?
Symmetric stalk/gland enlargement with homogeneous enhancement strongly suggests lymphocytic hypophysitis, IgG4-related hypophysitis, or granulomatous disease (e.g., sarcoidosis, Langerhans cell histiocytosis). The most urgent initial step is glucocorticoid replacement for corticotroph deficiency, which can be life-threatening; DDAVP for diabetes insipidus should follow. A targeted systemic work-up (ACE, IgG4, chest imaging, metabolic screen) should be initiated before empiric immunosuppression.

Prolactinoma assessment and management: an international multidisciplinary cross-sectional survey of current practice.

Notable
Pituitary · 2026

This international survey of 249 clinicians (210 endocrinologists, 39 neurosurgeons) across four continents mapped real-world prolactinoma practice against the 2023 Pituitary Society Consensus Statement. Despite guidelines supporting surgery as an equal first-line option in selected microprolactinomas, cabergoline remained overwhelmingly preferred in that setting. Only 20% of respondents routinely referred newly diagnosed cases for multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion, and resource gaps were notable—including inconsistent Knosp grade reporting and limited access to transcription factor immunohistochemistry. Neurosurgeons varied considerably on indications for surgery and views on dopamine agonist-induced tumor fibrosis.

Caveat: Survey-based data are subject to response bias, and self-reported practice may not accurately reflect actual clinical behavior; the neurosurgeon sample (n=39) is small and may not be representative internationally.
PMID 42749973 · doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01757-8
Case: According to the 2023 Pituitary Society Consensus Statement, surgery is considered an equal first-line option in selected cases of microprolactinoma. However, this survey found that most clinicians still preferred cabergoline in that scenario. For which patient profile might surgery be a genuinely preferred first-line option over dopamine agonist therapy in a microprolactinoma?
Per the 2023 Pituitary Society guidance (as reflected in this survey's framing), surgery may be preferred as first-line in selected microprolactinomas in patients who desire definitive cure and wish to avoid long-term medical therapy, those planning pregnancy who want to minimize drug exposure, or those intolerant of or non-adherent to dopamine agonists—provided the surgery is performed at an experienced center with acceptable cure rates for microadenomas.

The Sentinel Hormone GDF-15, Glucocorticoid Homeostasis and Adrenal Disorders.

Preliminary
Endocrine reviews · 2026

This review in Endocrine Reviews synthesizes emerging evidence that growth and differentiation factor 15 (GDF-15), a stress-responsive hormone signaling via the brainstem GFRAL receptor, interacts dynamically with the HPA axis. The authors propose that GDF-15 may act as a "sentinel hormone" capable of activating the HPA axis during cellular stress, with downstream glucocorticoid signaling potentially mediating some of GDF-15's known cardiovascular, metabolic, immune, and musculoskeletal effects. Additionally, the review discusses putative roles for GDF-15 in adrenal insufficiency (including as a biomarker or modifier of glucocorticoid replacement adequacy) and in adrenocortical carcinoma biology.

Caveat: This is a mechanistic and hypothesis-generating review; the proposed GDF-15–HPA axis interplay is largely based on preclinical and associative data, and no clinical trials or interventional data in adrenal disease are yet available. Findings should be interpreted as preliminary until prospective clinical studies are conducted.
PMID 42747392 · doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnag035
Case: A patient with known adrenal insufficiency on stable glucocorticoid replacement presents with unexplained weight loss, fatigue, and nausea. GDF-15 levels are markedly elevated. Based on the framework proposed in this review, what additional considerations does elevated GDF-15 raise in the context of HPA axis disease?
According to this review, elevated GDF-15 in a patient with adrenal insufficiency could reflect ongoing cellular stress and may signal inadequate glucocorticoid replacement or an intercurrent stress state activating the HPA axis. GDF-15 signals via GFRAL to suppress appetite and induce nausea/vomiting—symptoms that can mimic adrenal crisis or under-replacement. The review also raises the hypothesis that GDF-15 itself may modulate HPA axis activity, so elevated levels warrant reassessment of replacement adequacy and investigation for intercurrent illness, while recognizing the clinical evidence base remains preliminary.
