Acromegalic Osteoarthropathy: A New Paradigm of Pathological Musculoskeletal Remodeling.

Notable
European journal of endocrinology · 2026

This review proposes an integrative framework repositioning acromegalic musculoskeletal disease—traditionally treated as separate complications of skeletal fragility and arthropathy—as a unified disorder of pathological musculoskeletal remodeling involving bone, joints, muscle, and body composition. The authors argue that bone fragility, arthropathy, muscle dysfunction, and altered body composition share common biological mechanisms driven by GH/IGF-1 excess, and that these changes frequently persist even after successful biochemical remission. Practically, the review advocates shifting from endocrine-centered care to phenotype-driven, multidisciplinary surveillance with attention to fracture risk determinants beyond BMD.

Caveat: This is a narrative review without systematic meta-analysis or new primary data; recommendations for practice change remain expert-opinion level pending prospective longitudinal studies.
PMID 42736608 · doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag178
Case: A 52-year-old woman with acromegaly achieves biochemical remission (normal IGF-1) after transsphenoidal surgery. She reports progressive knee pain and back stiffness. Her DEXA is borderline normal. How should her musculoskeletal status be managed going forward?
According to this framework, biochemical remission does not halt acromegalic musculoskeletal remodeling; arthropathy, vertebral fractures, and muscle dysfunction can persist or progress independently. She warrants ongoing phenotype-driven surveillance including vertebral fracture assessment, arthropathy evaluation, and functional muscle assessment—not reassurance based on BMD or IGF-1 alone.

From Detection to Decision: A Single-Center Analysis of Preoperative Imaging, Surgical Indications and Postoperative Histopathological Findings in Adrenal Lesions.

Notable
Diagnostics (Basel, Switzerland) · 2026

In this retrospective series of 157 adrenalectomy patients, 9% of lesions ≥4 cm and 14% of lesions ≥6 cm were malignant on final pathology; notably, the malignancy rate rose to 22% for right-sided lesions ≥6 cm. All adrenocortical carcinomas exceeded 4 cm (mean 8.3 cm) and occurred exclusively on the right side in this cohort. Tumor size and right-sided localization were independently associated with malignancy on logistic regression, reinforcing current guideline thresholds while adding laterality as a potential clinical signal.

Caveat: Single-center retrospective design with 157 patients limits generalizability; the exclusive right-sided distribution of ACC may reflect selection/referral bias rather than a true biological predilection. The absence of imaging HU data and washout characteristics limits radiologic correlation.
PMID 42739237 · doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics16172807
Case: A 61-year-old man is referred for a right adrenal mass measuring 6.5 cm with HU 28 on non-contrast CT and indeterminate washout. He has no symptoms of hormone excess, but screening labs reveal mildly elevated 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines. Based on this study's findings, what is the approximate malignancy risk for a right-sided lesion ≥6 cm, and what are the management implications?
This study found a 22% malignancy rate for right-sided adrenal lesions ≥6 cm. Combined with indeterminate imaging characteristics and biochemical evidence of catecholamine excess (pheo must be excluded and treated first), surgical resection after appropriate alpha-blockade is strongly warranted—both to exclude/treat pheochromocytoma and to address the meaningful malignancy risk.

Treatment of growth hormone deficiency with long-acting growth hormones in children, adolescents and adults: Clinical evidence and practical considerations.

Notable
Annales d'endocrinologie · 2026

This review synthesizes clinical trial evidence for three once-weekly long-acting GH (LAGH) formulations now available in the EU—lonapegsomatropin, somatrogon, and somapacitan—all of which demonstrated non-inferiority to daily GH in annualized height velocity in children, with comparable safety profiles. Somapacitan is currently the only LAGH approved in France for both pediatric and adult GHD, making it the most clinically versatile option across the age spectrum. The review addresses practical dosing, monitoring, transition-period management, and special populations, highlighting that real-world adherence data and long-term metabolic outcomes remain under active study.

Caveat: Pivotal trials establishing non-inferiority were primarily growth velocity endpoints in children; long-term metabolic, bone health, and quality-of-life data for adults on LAGHs are limited and ongoing. Some trial data are industry-sponsored. EU approvals differ from other regulatory jurisdictions (e.g., FDA).
PMID 42727727 · doi.org/10.1016/j.ando.2026.103093
Case: A 14-year-old with childhood-onset GHD and documented poor adherence to daily GH injections is transitioning to adult care. His parents ask about once-weekly GH options. What does current clinical trial evidence show regarding efficacy of LAGH formulations compared to daily GH in children, and what is the key remaining evidence gap?
Clinical trials of once-weekly LAGHs (lonapegsomatropin, somatrogon, somapacitan) have demonstrated non-inferiority to daily GH for annualized height velocity in children with comparable safety. The key remaining evidence gap is long-term real-world data on metabolic outcomes, bone health, and sustained adherence benefits—particularly relevant during the transition to adult care.
