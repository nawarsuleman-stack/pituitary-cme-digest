Morning urinary cortisol-to-creatinine ratio by ECLIA: an accurate screen for Cushing syndrome.

Notable
Journal of the Endocrine Society · 2026

This prospective study of 126 patients evaluated the diagnostic performance of an ECLIA-based morning first-void urinary cortisol-to-creatinine ratio (UCCR) for Cushing syndrome screening. At a cutoff of 18.6 nmol/mmol, the UCCR achieved sensitivity of 84.4%, specificity of 94.6%, and NPV of 94.7%, with an AUC of 0.963 — essentially on par with 24-hour UFC (AUC 0.95) and comparable (though somewhat inferior) to late-night salivary cortisol (AUC 0.996). The authors propose that ECLIA-based morning UCCR, in combination with LNSC, could replace the cumbersome 24-hour UFC collection in many clinical settings, particularly where LC-MS/MS is unavailable.

Caveat: Single-center study with 32 confirmed CS cases (25.4%), so the sample is relatively small and selection criteria favored patients with clinical suspicion. ECLIA platforms are known to have cross-reactivity with cortisol metabolites, and the cutoff may not generalize across laboratories or patient populations without local validation.

Coexistence of primary aldosteronism and hypercortisolism in patients investigated for secondary hypertension and/or adrenal incidentaloma.

Notable
BMC endocrine disorders · 2026

In this retrospective cohort of 110 patients with confirmed primary aldosteronism (PA), 8.1% had overt Cushing's syndrome and 10% had mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS), giving a combined hypercortisolism prevalence of ~18%. Coexistence of hypercortisolism was associated with significantly higher rates of diabetes mellitus (45% vs. 21%), higher mean HbA1c, and more pronounced hypokalemia. These findings reinforce the clinical importance of routinely screening PA patients — particularly those with adrenal masses — for glucocorticoid co-secretion, as the metabolic burden appears additive.

Caveat: Retrospective single-center design with a relatively small cohort (n=110) limits generalizability. The definition of MACS used and the thresholds applied for 1-mg DST suppression are not fully detailed in the abstract, and selection bias toward higher-risk patients referred to a tertiary center is likely.
PMID 43053005 · doi.org/10.1186/s12902-026-02504-7
Case: You confirm primary aldosteronism in a 55-year-old hypertensive man with a 2.5 cm right adrenal adenoma. His HbA1c is 6.4% and fasting glucose is borderline elevated. Should you pursue further workup for glucocorticoid co-secretion, and what does this study suggest about the prevalence and metabolic consequences of doing so?
Yes. This study found that approximately 18% of PA patients had concomitant hypercortisolism (8.1% overt CS, 10% MACS). Those with co-existing hypercortisolism had significantly higher rates of diabetes (45% vs. 21%) and higher HbA1c, supporting routine screening for MACS with a 1-mg overnight DST in all PA patients, especially those with an adrenal mass.

French AFU Cancer Committee Guideline - Update 2026-2028: Adrenal incidentalomas and adrenal cancers.

Notable
The French journal of urology · 2026

This updated 2026 French (CCAFU) practice guideline covers the full workup and management pathway for adrenal incidentalomas and malignant adrenal tumors (adrenocortical carcinoma, malignant pheochromocytoma, and adrenal metastases). Key emphases include mandatory biochemical/hormonal assessment for subclinical secretion in all incidentalomas, risk-stratified decisions on surveillance frequency vs. surgery based on imaging and functional characteristics, and referral of all malignant adrenal tumors to the COMETE expert network — with explicit statement that management errors are a significant driver of life-threatening outcomes. Hereditary origins of ACC and malignant pheochromocytoma are highlighted as requiring consideration even in apparently sporadic cases.

Caveat: This is a national French guideline; referral pathway recommendations (e.g., COMETE network) are France-specific and may not map directly to other healthcare systems. The abstract does not detail the evidence grading for individual recommendations, limiting assessment of the underlying evidence quality.
PMID 43051712 · doi.org/10.1016/j.fjurol.2026.103227
Case: A 58-year-old woman is found to have a 4.2 cm unenhanced CT attenuation of 22 HU adrenal mass discovered incidentally during abdominal imaging for a GI complaint. She is otherwise well and normotensive. Per these updated 2026 guidelines, what are the two essential initial workup steps?
Per the CCAFU 2026 guideline, all adrenal incidentalomas require (1) full biochemical/hormonal assessment to evaluate for subclinical hormone secretion (including screening for MACS, pheochromocytoma, and primary aldosteronism if hypertensive), and (2) morphological characterization (CT attenuation, washout characteristics, size) to assess malignancy risk — with subsequent management (surveillance vs. surgery) guided by the integrated findings of this initial assessment.
