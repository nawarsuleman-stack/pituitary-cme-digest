Long-term post-trial extension of osilodrostat therapy in selected Canadian patients with Cushing disease from the LINC 3 and LINC 4 programs.

Notable
Frontiers in endocrinology · 2026

This retrospective multicenter analysis followed 11 Canadian patients with Cushing disease who continued osilodrostat after completing the LINC 3 or LINC 4 trials, with 7 remaining on therapy long-term. Six of those 7 had controlled mean urinary free cortisol at last follow-up after a median exposure of 98 months (range 83–121 months), supporting durable biochemical control. Maintenance doses varied widely (median 6 mg/day, range 0.125–40 mg/day), and several patients required progressive dose down-titration over time, consistent with evolving sensitivity. Two patients experienced clinically significant pituitary tumor enlargement requiring discontinuation and surgery, underscoring that tumor surveillance remains mandatory during long-term steroidogenesis inhibitor therapy.

Caveat: Highly selected cohort of only 11 patients continuing from industry-sponsored trials; 4 discontinued before the extension phase, introducing survivorship bias. Results are descriptive without inferential statistics, and this experience may not generalize to patients outside specialized centers or those who did not tolerate early therapy.
PMID 42935875 · doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1902818
Case: A 42-year-old woman with Cushing disease achieves biochemical control on osilodrostat 10 mg/day after failing transsphenoidal surgery. Five years into therapy her dose has been down-titrated to 2 mg/day to avoid hypocortisolism. What additional surveillance consideration does this long-term real-world cohort highlight beyond cortisol monitoring?
Clinically significant pituitary tumor enlargement occurred in 2 of 11 patients in this cohort, leading to discontinuation and surgery. Ongoing pituitary MRI surveillance is essential for patients on long-term osilodrostat, as tumor growth can occur even during sustained biochemical control.

An Overview of Cushing's Syndrome Caused by Ectopic Adrenocorticotropin Secretion.

Notable
Journal of clinical medicine · 2026

This comprehensive review covers the full clinical and diagnostic landscape of ectopic Cushing's syndrome (ECS), which accounts for 10–20% of endogenous hypercortisolism. It delineates two phenotypic patterns: an aggressive presentation driven by rapidly growing tumors (e.g., small-cell lung carcinoma) featuring severe hypokalemia, myopathy, and hyperpigmentation without typical centripetal obesity, versus an indolent presentation from well-differentiated bronchial carcinoids that may closely mimic Cushing's disease. Bilateral inferior petrosal sinus sampling (BIPSS) is highlighted as the gold standard for distinguishing pituitary from ectopic ACTH sources, though the review emphasizes that absence of a central-to-peripheral gradient supports—but does not itself localize—an ectopic source. Medical control of hypercortisolism with steroidogenesis inhibitors (metyrapone, ketoconazole, osilodrostat) or bilateral adrenalectomy is framed as a critical bridge while tumor localization is pursued, given that 10–20% of ectopic tumors remain occult on multimodality imaging.

Caveat: This is a narrative review without systematic methodology or pooled data; clinical heterogeneity across cited studies is substantial. Recommendations on steroidogenesis inhibitor choice are not derived from head-to-head trials.
PMID 42941705 · doi.org/10.3390/jcm15208192
Case: A 58-year-old man presents with 3 weeks of profound proximal weakness, blood pressure 180/110 mmHg, serum potassium 2.8 mEq/L, and markedly elevated 24-hour urinary free cortisol. He lacks centripetal obesity. ACTH is 280 pg/mL. BIPSS shows a central-to-peripheral ACTH ratio of 1.4 (below the 2.0 cutoff). What is the most likely diagnosis, and what is the priority next step?
The clinical picture (acute severe hypercortisolism, hypokalemia, myopathy, absent centripetal obesity) combined with an elevated ACTH and a non-suppressed central-to-peripheral ACTH ratio on BIPSS is consistent with ectopic ACTH syndrome. The priority next step is multimodality tumor localization (high-resolution CT of chest/abdomen/pelvis followed by Ga-68 DOTATATE PET/CT) while initiating urgent medical adrenal blockade to control hypercortisolism and prevent life-threatening complications.

Mild Autonomous Cortisol Secretion Is Associated with Computed Tomography-Derived Hepatic Steatosis in Patients with Adrenal Incidentaloma.

Preliminary
Medicina (Kaunas, Lithuania) · 2026

In this retrospective single-center study of 123 patients with adrenal incidentaloma (39 MACS, 84 NFAI), MACS was associated with subtle CT-derived hepatic fat accumulation compared to non-functioning adenomas, most clearly reflected in the liver-spleen attenuation difference (p = 0.048). Conventional CT-defined steatosis thresholds and non-invasive metabolic indices did not differ significantly between groups, suggesting that continuous CT attenuation parameters may be more sensitive for detecting subclinical hepatic lipid accumulation. Of the biochemical cortisol markers tested, only 24-hour urinary free cortisol correlated significantly with CT-derived hepatic attenuation parameters, including the liver-spleen attenuation difference after multivariable adjustment.

Caveat: Retrospective design, modest sample size (especially the MACS group, n=39), and cross-sectional nature preclude causal inference. The borderline statistical thresholds (some p-values at 0.047–0.056) and lack of histologic validation (liver biopsy) limit confidence; prospective studies are needed before this influences routine practice.
PMID 42942541 · doi.org/10.3390/medicina62102010
Case: A 55-year-old woman is referred for an incidentally found 2.3 cm lipid-rich left adrenal nodule. Her 1-mg overnight DST cortisol is 2.8 µg/dL (reference <1.8). She has no overt Cushingoid features but has BMI 29, mild hypertension, and borderline fasting glucose. Routine abdominal CT shows a liver attenuation of 48 HU and spleen attenuation of 62 HU. How does this imaging finding relate to her adrenal diagnosis?
The liver-spleen attenuation difference is negative (liver 48 HU vs. spleen 62 HU), consistent with hepatic steatosis. According to this study, MACS—which her post-DST cortisol of 2.8 µg/dL suggests—is associated with subtle CT-derived hepatic fat accumulation, most sensitively detected by the liver-spleen attenuation difference rather than conventional steatosis thresholds. This reinforces the metabolic consequences of even mild autonomous cortisol excess and supports comprehensive cardiometabolic evaluation in MACS patients.
