Proteomic profiling of somatotropinoma subtypes reveals TNIP1 overexpression in sparsely granulated tumors in a small discovery cohort.

Preliminary
Pituitary · 2026

This study used quantitative mass spectrometry on densely granulated (DG) versus sparsely granulated (SG) somatotroph adenomas and identified TNIP1 (TNFAIP3-interacting protein 1) as the most significantly overexpressed protein in SG tumors. Higher TNIP1 expression correlated with larger tumor volume, higher Knosp grade, elevated IGF-1, and SSTR5 expression — features already associated with poor first-generation SSA response. COQ6 showed the inverse pattern, negatively correlating with tumor size and Knosp grade. The findings provide a molecular basis for phenotypic differences between granulation subtypes and may point toward novel therapeutic targets.

Caveat: Extremely small discovery cohort (n=3 per subtype for proteomics); IHC validation extended to 64 tumors but the study remains hypothesis-generating. Functional significance of TNIP1 in somatotroph biology is unproven.
PMID 42927587 · doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01778-3
Case: A 45-year-old with acromegaly has a sparsely granulated GH adenoma on pathology, Knosp grade 3, and IGF-1 at 2.8× ULN. Based on this proteomic study, which protein is most likely overexpressed in this tumor compared to a densely granulated adenoma, and what clinical implication does this carry?
TNIP1 is the most significantly overexpressed protein in sparsely granulated somatotroph adenomas in this dataset. Its overexpression correlates with larger, more invasive tumors with higher SSTR5 expression, potentially supporting selection of pasireotide (a multi-receptor SSA with SSTR5 activity) in patients with sparsely granulated tumors who are unlikely to respond to first-generation SSAs.

Personalised acromegaly treatment: implementing precision medicine in the Gulf States.

Notable
Frontiers in endocrinology · 2026

This review highlights that biochemical control rates with first-generation SRLs in Gulf region acromegaly cohorts are substantially lower (30–40%) than in European populations, attributed to a higher burden of macroadenomas, metabolic comorbidities, and delayed diagnosis. The authors advocate for upfront use of MRI signal characteristics and tumor granulation patterns as practical, widely available biomarkers to guide first-line therapy selection, including earlier use of pasireotide in patients predicted to respond poorly to octreotide/lanreotide. A practical biomarker-guided treatment algorithm tailored to Gulf healthcare systems is proposed.

Caveat: This is a narrative review without new primary data; the proposed algorithm is expert opinion adapted from existing biomarker literature. Generalizability beyond Gulf/Middle Eastern populations is uncertain, and head-to-head RCT evidence for biomarker-driven first-line pasireotide selection remains limited.
PMID 42947680 · doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1951420
Case: A 52-year-old man in your clinic has a newly diagnosed GH-secreting macroadenoma. MRI shows a T2-hypointense signal. He has poorly controlled type 2 diabetes. How does the granulation pattern/MRI signal inform your choice between first-generation SRLs and pasireotide as initial medical therapy?
T2-hypointense signal on MRI correlates with densely granulated somatotroph tumors, which are more likely to respond to first-generation SRLs. Sparsely granulated tumors tend to be T2-hyperintense and respond poorly to octreotide/lanreotide, making pasireotide a better-supported choice in that subgroup. However, pasireotide's propensity to worsen hyperglycemia must be weighed carefully in a patient with pre-existing diabetes, regardless of granulation pattern.

Pulmonary edema during cesarean delivery secondary to Takotsubo syndrome revealing pheochromocytoma: a case report.

Notable
AJOG global reports · 2026

A 35-year-old woman developed acute pulmonary edema with severe hypertension (205/130 mmHg) and a reduced LVEF of 35% with apical ballooning during cesarean delivery under spinal anesthesia — consistent with Takotsubo syndrome (TTS). Point-of-care ultrasound guided the acute diagnosis, and subsequent CT identified a right adrenal mass; urinary catecholamine metabolites confirmed pheochromocytoma. LVEF recovered to 57% within one week, highlighting the reversibility of catecholamine-induced cardiomyopathy when the underlying cause is identified and addressed promptly.

Caveat: Single case report; cannot establish incidence or generalize management. Importantly, this case underscores a known but underappreciated pitfall: peripartum hemodynamic crises and TTS should trigger adrenal evaluation, yet spinal anesthesia itself may have contributed to catecholamine surge in an unprepared patient.
PMID 42935808 · doi.org/10.1016/j.xagr.2026.100700
Case: A 35-year-old pregnant woman develops severe hypertension and acute pulmonary edema with echocardiographic apical ballooning during cesarean section. After stabilization, what is the most critical next step in the diagnostic workup that could identify a surgically correctable cause?
Measurement of plasma or urine catecholamines/metanephrines and cross-sectional adrenal imaging (CT or MRI) to exclude pheochromocytoma, as demonstrated in this case where TTS during delivery was the unmasking event for an occult pheo. Prompt identification is essential because subsequent anesthetic or surgical procedures without appropriate alpha-blockade carry life-threatening risk.
