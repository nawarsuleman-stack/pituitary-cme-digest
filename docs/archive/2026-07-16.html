Machine learning quantifies within-subject facial change in acromegaly.

Notable
European journal of endocrinology · 2026

In this multicenter international cohort study, a face-recognition machine-learning model was able to detect measurable within-person facial shifts toward an acromegaly-associated pattern over time, with 76% of acromegaly patients moving in the predicted direction (P=0.0003) versus no significant shift in aging controls. The model achieved an AUC of 0.858 separating advanced acromegaly from controls on held-out photographs, and prediagnosis untreated baselines already showed modest separation from controls (AUC 0.65), raising the possibility of earlier recognition from ordinary photographs. This approach could eventually complement clinical screening by flagging subtle acromegaloid facial evolution before overt diagnosis, particularly in the primary care setting.

Caveat: The sample with two usable timepoints was small (n=69), the longitudinal contrast strata trend was not statistically significant (P=0.15), and the study was cross-sectional/retrospective in design — prospective validation with standardized photography protocols is needed before any clinical deployment.
PMID 42886865 · doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag189
Case: A 38-year-old patient presents with subtle coarsening of facial features over the past 4 years. IGF-1 is mildly elevated at 1.2× ULN; random GH is 1.1 ng/mL. Based on the machine-learning facial-change study published in EJE 2026, which statement best describes the potential utility of longitudinal photograph-based analysis in this scenario?
The study demonstrated that acromegaly-associated facial change is measurable within individuals from ordinary photographs using a face-recognition model, and prediagnosis untreated faces already showed modest separation from controls (AUC 0.65). This suggests that longitudinal photograph-based scoring could serve as a non-invasive adjunct to biochemical testing to support earlier recognition, though it is not yet validated for clinical use and requires prospective confirmation.

Comparison of demographic, clinical and follow-up data of patients with micromegaly and acromegaly: a retrospective analysis from a single tertiary center.

Notable
BMC endocrine disorders · 2026

In this single-center retrospective review of 117 acromegaly patients, 14 (12%) met criteria for 'micromegaly' — defined by IGF-1 above ULN with GH <2 ng/mL and/or suppressed GH nadir <1 ng/mL plus classic clinical features. Despite lower GH levels and smaller, more often microadenoma-sized tumors, patients with micromegaly had comparable comorbidity profiles and clinical manifestations to classical acromegaly, underscoring that biochemically milder GH excess does not equate to clinically milder disease. Micromegaly patients had higher early postoperative biochemical control rates, but long-term remission rates were similar to classical acromegaly.

Caveat: Single-center retrospective design with a small micromegaly subgroup (n=14) limits generalizability. The biochemical definition of micromegaly lacks consensus standardization, and sensitivity analyses using alternative definitions were explored but a universally agreed threshold does not yet exist.
PMID 42928242 · doi.org/10.1186/s12902-026-02444-2
Case: A 45-year-old woman has classic acromegaloid features — enlarged hands, prognathism, and hyperhidrosis — with IGF-1 at 1.4× ULN but a GH nadir of 0.8 ng/mL on OGTT and a baseline GH of 1.6 ng/mL. Imaging shows a 6 mm pituitary microadenoma. Based on the micromegaly study, what should guide your management approach?
This presentation is consistent with 'micromegaly.' The study found that despite lower GH levels and smaller tumors, these patients have comorbidity and symptom burdens comparable to classical acromegaly. Apparently low GH does not indicate clinically mild disease, so full evaluation and treatment (including surgical consideration) remain warranted, similar to the approach for classical acromegaly.

Prevention and management of hormonal crisis in neuroendocrine tumors undergoing PRRT (peptide receptor radionuclide therapy): a systematic review.

Notable
ESMO gastrointestinal oncology · 2026

This systematic review of 28 studies examined hormonal crises during PRRT for functioning NETs, including carcinoid crisis (~1% incidence in available cohorts), insulinoma-related hypoglycemia, VIPoma crises, ectopic Cushing syndrome exacerbation, and catecholaminergic crisis in pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma. Hypoglycemic crises in insulinoma patients clustered around the first PRRT cycle, supporting inpatient glucose monitoring at that stage. Evidence across all syndromes was limited and heterogeneous, but consistently pointed toward pretreatment endocrine optimization, syndrome-specific prophylaxis, and multidisciplinary coordination as key safety pillars.

Caveat: The evidence base is heavily case-report/retrospective-series dependent (18/28 studies); definitions of 'hormonal crisis' varied widely across studies, making incidence estimates unreliable. No firm protocol recommendations are possible from current data, and pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma crises during PRRT are represented by only small series.
PMID 42927326 · doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100428
Case: You are preparing a patient with a functioning paraganglioma and somatostatin-receptor-positive metastatic disease for PRRT. Based on the 2026 systematic review on hormonal crises during PRRT, what pre-treatment considerations are most important for this patient's safety?
The review identified catecholaminergic crisis as a recognized, albeit rare, complication of PRRT in pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma patients. Evidence — though limited to small series — consistently emphasized pretreatment endocrine optimization (adequate alpha-blockade, hemodynamic preparation), syndrome-specific prophylaxis, early recognition protocols, and multidisciplinary management. Structured risk assessment and periprocedural monitoring are considered essential, as harmonized management algorithms are not yet established.
