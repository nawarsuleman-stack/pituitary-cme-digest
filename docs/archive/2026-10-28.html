Immunotherapy mediated pituitary toxicity: current knowledge and priorities for care.

Practice-changing
Endocrine reviews · 2026

This comprehensive review in Endocrine Reviews synthesizes current evidence on immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-related pituitary toxicity, emphasizing that the condition remains clinically underappreciated due to its subtle, variable phenotype and significant overlap with other treatment-related effects. The authors outline multiple plausible immune pathways — including activation of pre-existing autoimmunity, direct checkpoint-mediated injury, paraneoplastic responses, and cytokine-driven damage — none of which dominates across patients. The review highlights that validated biomarkers are still lacking and stresses the growing importance of long-term endocrine follow-up and patient education as ICI-treated survivors increase in number. Clinically, adrenal insufficiency remains the most dangerous acute manifestation, and practitioners should maintain a low threshold for HPA axis assessment in any ICI-treated patient with nonspecific symptoms.

Caveat: This is a narrative review without a formal systematic methodology or meta-analysis; evidence quality is heterogeneous across the cited studies, and no validated diagnostic or management algorithm is yet established.
PMID 43025976 · doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnag041
Case: A 58-year-old man with metastatic melanoma on pembrolizumab presents with 3 weeks of fatigue, nausea, and mild hyponatremia. TSH is normal. What pituitary irAE should be highest on your differential, and what is the most time-sensitive hormonal assessment to obtain?
ICI-related hypophysitis with secondary adrenal insufficiency should be highest on the differential. The most time-sensitive assessment is an 8 AM serum cortisol (and ACTH), as central adrenal insufficiency is potentially life-threatening and may be present even when TSH is normal — the pituitary irAE phenotype does not reliably cause panhypopituitarism in a predictable sequence.

Predictors of early and 6-month remission after transsphenoidal surgery in Cushing's disease: a cohort study of 100 patients.

Notable
Endocrine · 2026

In this cohort of 100 patients with confirmed Cushing's disease undergoing transsphenoidal surgery, early remission was achieved in 58% (cortisol <1.8 µg/dL) or 80% (cortisol <5 µg/dL), with 84% reaching 6-month remission. Postoperative first-week serum cortisol and ACTH levels, residual tumor on MRI, and macroadenoma status were the most consistent predictors of remission, while preoperative clinical and biochemical parameters — including ODST, LDDST, HDDST, and BIPSS — showed limited predictive utility (AUC <0.70). Notably, patients who achieved 6-month remission required a mean of 30 months of glucocorticoid replacement, reflecting sustained HPA axis suppression as a marker of completeness of resection. These findings reinforce the primacy of early postoperative cortisol nadir as the key remission indicator.

Caveat: This is a single-center cohort with univariate logistic regression only; the authors explicitly note that multivariable analysis is warranted. Sample size is modest (n=100) and selection/referral bias at a single institution may limit generalizability.
PMID 43024123 · doi.org/10.1007/s12020-026-04841-y
Case: A 34-year-old woman undergoes transsphenoidal surgery for Cushing's disease. On postoperative day 4, her serum cortisol is 6.2 µg/dL and her ACTH is 28 pg/mL; postoperative MRI shows no visible residual tumor. How should you interpret this, and what does this cohort study suggest about her likelihood of 6-month remission?
A cortisol of 6.2 µg/dL exceeds the strict <1.8 µg/dL early remission threshold but falls above the <5 µg/dL threshold as well, placing her in an intermediate zone. Per this study, residual tumor on MRI and elevated postoperative ACTH were the strongest negative predictors of remission; with a clear postoperative MRI, her 6-month remission probability is relatively favorable, but continued close biochemical monitoring is essential. Preoperative tests such as ODST or BIPSS did not add predictive value.

Germline AIP Testing in Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors: Diagnostic Yield, Variant Interpretation, and a Phenotype-Driven Clinical Approach-A Narrative Review.

Notable
Diagnostics (Basel, Switzerland) · 2026

This narrative review critically evaluates when and how to pursue germline AIP testing in pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNETs), synthesizing evidence from January 2006 to April 2025. The highest diagnostic yield is consistently seen in familial isolated pituitary adenomas (FIPA), pituitary gigantism, and childhood- or young-onset GH-secreting macroadenomas. Importantly, contemporary ACMG/AMP variant classification standards have refined and generally lowered estimates of clinically actionable AIP variants compared to older literature, cautioning against over-interpretation of variants of uncertain significance. The review supports a phenotype-driven rather than universal testing approach, with multigene panels reserved for cases with atypical features or persistent clinical suspicion after negative targeted AIP testing.

Caveat: This is a narrative rather than systematic review, introducing potential selection bias in the literature included. The conclusions regarding variant reclassification rest largely on retrospective cohort data and expert consensus rather than prospective validation studies.
PMID 43028345 · doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics16213471
Case: A 17-year-old male presents with rapid height gain and is found to have a GH-secreting macroadenoma. Family history is negative for pituitary tumors. Should germline AIP testing be offered, and what is the anticipated yield?
Yes — young-onset GH-secreting macroadenomas are among the highest-yield indications for AIP testing, even in the absence of a family history, as de novo AIP variants occur. This review confirms that this phenotype (childhood/young-onset GH macroadenoma) consistently shows the highest diagnostic yield. A negative result should prompt consideration of multigene panel testing if clinical suspicion remains.
