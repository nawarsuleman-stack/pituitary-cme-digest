Hypofractionated radiosurgery for pituitary adenomas: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Notable
Neurosurgical review · 2026

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 18 studies (785 patients) found that hypofractionated stereotactic radiosurgery (HFSRS) achieved tumor control in 98% of pituitary adenomas at last follow-up, with visual worsening in only 2% and new persistent hypopituitarism in 6%. Endocrine remission in functioning adenomas was 46% — consistent with single-fraction SRS benchmarks. These pooled data support HFSRS as a viable, safe option particularly when optic apparatus proximity precludes single-fraction SRS.

Caveat: Only 785 patients across 18 mostly retrospective studies with heterogeneous prescribed doses (15–35 Gy in 2–5 fractions), platforms, and follow-up durations; no prospective head-to-head comparison with single-fraction SRS exists. The 46% endocrine remission rate should be interpreted cautiously given varying remission definitions across included studies.
PMID 43037715 · doi.org/10.1007/s10143-026-04592-4
Case: A 54-year-old woman has a residual Cushing's disease macroadenoma abutting the optic chiasm at less than 2 mm following transsphenoidal surgery, precluding standard single-fraction radiosurgery. Her neurosurgeon asks about hypofractionated SRS. Based on pooled data from this meta-analysis, what tumor control rate and new hypopituitarism risk should you quote her?
Pooled tumor control with HFSRS is approximately 98% (95% CI 96–99%), with new persistent hypopituitarism in about 6% of patients. Visual worsening occurred in only 2%, supporting its use in this anatomically constrained setting, though endocrine remission of the functioning tumor was achieved in only ~46% of functioning adenoma cases.

Neuronavigation-assisted versus conventional endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery for pituitary adenomas: a pilot randomized controlled trial.

Preliminary
Pituitary · 2026

This pilot RCT (n=50, 25 per arm) comparing neuronavigation-assisted versus conventional endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery found no statistically significant difference in gross total resection rates (28% vs. 16%; adjusted OR 2.66, 95% CI 0.41–17.06), though a numerical trend favored navigation. Blood loss was significantly lower with navigation (median 250 vs. 350 mL; p=0.016). No giant adenoma achieved gross total resection regardless of technique.

Caveat: This is an explicitly underpowered pilot trial from a single center (Ain Shams University) with 25 patients per arm — far too small to draw definitive conclusions about efficacy. The trial was registered retrospectively, introducing potential bias. The blood-loss finding is hypothesis-generating only.
PMID 43026363 · doi.org/10.1007/s11102-026-01772-9
Case: Your neurosurgical colleague asks whether intraoperative neuronavigation should become routine for all endoscopic transsphenoidal pituitary surgeries based on current RCT evidence. What is the most accurate response?
Current RCT evidence remains insufficient to mandate routine neuronavigation. This pilot trial showed only a non-significant numerical trend toward higher gross total resection with navigation (adjusted OR 2.66, CI crossing 1.0) and a statistically significant but hypothesis-generating reduction in blood loss. A definitive multicenter RCT with adequate power is still needed.

Adrenal Insufficiency After Preoperative Enfortumab Vedotin Plus Pembrolizumab in Locally Advanced Bladder Cancer: A Case Report.

Notable
IJU case reports · 2026

This case report describes central adrenal insufficiency (low ACTH and cortisol) presenting 25 days after radical cystectomy in a patient who had received four cycles of enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab for bladder cancer. The postoperative presentation with fever, anorexia, and fatigue initially mimicked a surgical complication, but prompt endocrine evaluation revealed the irAE etiology, and corticosteroid replacement produced rapid recovery. The case highlights the perioperative diagnostic pitfall of checkpoint inhibitor–induced adrenal insufficiency occurring well into the postoperative period.

Caveat: Single case report; causality between enfortumab vedotin versus pembrolizumab versus the combination cannot be established. The low ACTH pattern points to central (secondary) adrenal insufficiency, though the abstract does not detail pituitary imaging or further axis evaluation to exclude hypophysitis.
PMID 43041208 · doi.org/10.1002/iju5.70292
Case: A 72-year-old man who completed neoadjuvant enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab presents 3 weeks after radical cystectomy with fatigue, low-grade fever, and anorexia. Wound healing appears intact and cultures are negative. What endocrine diagnosis must be actively excluded, and what is the initial biochemical evaluation?
Immune checkpoint inhibitor–related adrenal insufficiency must be excluded. Initial evaluation should include an 8 AM serum cortisol and plasma ACTH. Low cortisol with low or inappropriately normal ACTH suggests central (secondary) adrenal insufficiency from pembrolizumab-induced hypophysitis or primary adrenal insufficiency from direct adrenal irAE; a cosyntropin stimulation test can further characterize adrenal reserve. Empiric corticosteroid replacement is appropriate if the clinical suspicion is high and the patient is hemodynamically compromised.
