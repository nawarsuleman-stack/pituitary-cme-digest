Clinical contribution of the PANOMEN-3 classification in predicting prognosis in pituitary neuroendocrine tumor.

Notable
Annales d'endocrinologie · 2026

This single-center Portuguese cohort of 92 surgically treated PitNETs validated the PANOMEN-3 postoperative risk classification, showing strongly graded progression-free survival by score: median PFS was not reached for grades 0–1, fell to 43 months for grade 2, and collapsed to just 5 months for grade 3. On multivariate Cox regression, grade 2 conferred a >6-fold and grade 3 a ~15-fold increase in progression risk versus grades 0–1 combined. The tool integrates clinical, biochemical, radiological, and histopathological variables into a practical postoperative surveillance framework. These data support incorporating PANOMEN-3 grading into routine postoperative planning for PitNETs.

Caveat: Retrospective, single-center design with only 92 patients and relatively few grade 3 tumors (n=9); external multicenter validation is needed before broad adoption. Median follow-up of 69 months is reasonable but longer follow-up may shift PFS estimates, particularly for grade 2.
PMID 42951065 · doi.org/10.1016/j.ando.2026.103116
Case: A 52-year-old woman undergoes transsphenoidal resection of a PitNET. Postoperative assessment places her in PANOMEN-3 grade 2. Based on this cohort's findings, what is her approximate median progression-free survival, and how does her risk compare to grade 0–1 patients?
Median PFS for PANOMEN-3 grade 2 was approximately 43 months, with a greater than 6-fold increase in progression risk (HR ~6.13) compared to grades 0–1 combined. This would justify more intensive postoperative imaging and biochemical surveillance intervals than for lower-grade tumors.

Diagnostic delay length and disease severity are the main predictors of sleep apnea in acromegaly patients.

Notable
Frontiers in endocrinology · 2026

In this prospective single-center study of 36 acromegaly patients undergoing objective polygraphy, OSA prevalence was 47.2%, predominantly mild. Diagnostic delay was the strongest independent predictor of both apnea-hypopnea index and oxygen desaturation index, outperforming BMI and disease control status. Patients with uncontrolled acromegaly had significantly more obstructive apneas and worse oxygenation parameters than those in remission. Notably, the Epworth Sleepiness Scale did not discriminate OSA status, underscoring that subjective sleepiness tools are insufficient for screening in this population.

Caveat: Small cohort (n=36) from a single center limits statistical power; the cross-sectional nature precludes establishing causality between diagnostic delay and OSA development. Residual confounding by comorbidities (e.g., hypertension, insulin resistance) is possible despite multivariate adjustment.
PMID 42936300 · doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1928818
Case: A 58-year-old man is newly diagnosed with acromegaly after a 6-year diagnostic delay. He denies excessive daytime sleepiness and his Epworth score is 7. Should he be referred for objective sleep evaluation, and what does this study suggest about using subjective sleepiness tools in acromegaly?
Yes, objective polygraphy (or polysomnography) should be obtained regardless of subjective sleepiness. This study found that Epworth Sleepiness Scale scores did not differ between acromegaly patients with and without OSA (p=0.96), making it an unreliable screening tool. Long diagnostic delay was the strongest predictor of OSA severity, so this patient is at elevated risk and warrants objective sleep testing.

"Paraneoplastic syndromes secondary to Νeuroendocrine Νeoplasms".

Notable
Endocrine reviews · 2026

This comprehensive review in Endocrine Reviews synthesizes the pathophysiology, clinical spectrum, diagnostic approach, and management of paraneoplastic syndromes (PNS) arising from gastroenteropancreatic and lung neuroendocrine neoplasms. Endocrine PNS — including ectopic ACTH secretion, carcinoid syndrome, paraneoplastic hypercalcemia, SIADH, hypoglycemia, and ectopic acromegaly — constitute the majority of cases and may precede or herald the primary tumor. The review highlights advances in molecular biology (neuroendocrine lineage plasticity, epigenetic reprogramming, aberrant prohormone processing) and improved diagnostic tools (liquid biopsy, functional imaging, molecular profiling) that enhance detection. Management requires coordinated syndrome-directed and tumor-targeted therapies including SSAs, PRRT, and emerging immunotherapeutic strategies.

Caveat: As a narrative review, this paper does not provide new primary data; recommendations reflect synthesis of existing evidence with acknowledged gaps in epidemiology, prognostic biomarkers, and optimal treatment sequencing. The true prevalence of PNS in NENs is stated to be underestimated, limiting any quantitative guidance.
PMID 42931764 · doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnag042
Case: A 44-year-old woman presents with hypokalemia, proximal myopathy, severe hyperglycemia, and hypertension. CT chest reveals a 2.1 cm pulmonary carcinoid. Plasma ACTH is markedly elevated and not suppressed by high-dose dexamethasone. What syndrome should be prioritized, and what is its significance in the context of NEN-related paraneoplastic disease?
Ectopic ACTH secretion causing Cushing's syndrome is the diagnosis. Per this review, ectopic ACTH secretion is one of the most clinically significant endocrine paraneoplastic syndromes associated with neuroendocrine neoplasms, including pulmonary carcinoids, and may precede or coincide with the tumor diagnosis. Prompt recognition is essential because hypercortisolism-related complications (infection, hyperglycemia, hypertension, hypokalemia) dominate morbidity and require syndrome-directed therapy (steroidogenesis inhibitors or bilateral adrenalectomy) alongside tumor-targeted treatment.
