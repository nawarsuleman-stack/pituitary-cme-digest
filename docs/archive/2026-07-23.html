Clinical course and management of adrenal endocrine hypertension: A single-center experience.

Notable
Endocrine regulations · 2026

This retrospective single-center study of 55 patients with adrenal causes of secondary hypertension (PA 52.8%, PHEO 29.1%, ACTH-independent Cushing's 18.1%) found notably low rates of complete hypertension remission after definitive treatment: only 21.8% after adrenalectomy for PA, 37.5% for PHEO, and 40% for Cushing's. Patients with PA were diagnosed youngest and had the most severe, resistant hypertension requiring the most antihypertensive agents. PHEO patients were oldest at diagnosis and had more paroxysmal, severe hypertension, while CS patients had the least severe hypertensive phenotype and least end-organ damage. The data reinforce that adrenal endocrine hypertension carries significant residual cardiovascular risk even after cure, underscoring the importance of continued antihypertensive management post-operatively.

Caveat: Small single-center retrospective cohort (n=55) with significant heterogeneity across three distinct adrenal etiologies; limited statistical power for subgroup comparisons, and no long-term cardiovascular outcome data beyond HTN remission rates.
PMID 42762504 · doi.org/10.2478/enr-2026-0023
Case: A 38-year-old woman with primary aldosteronism undergoes successful unilateral adrenalectomy with confirmed biochemical cure (normalized aldosterone-renin ratio). Her preoperative blood pressure required three antihypertensive agents. Based on the best available evidence, what is the most realistic expectation regarding her hypertension after surgery?
Complete hypertension remission occurs in only roughly 22% of surgically treated PA patients; most will still require antihypertensive therapy postoperatively, though the number of agents and BP control typically improve. Patients and clinicians should be counseled that biochemical cure does not equate to hypertension cure, and ongoing BP management remains essential.

Effectiveness of transsphenoidal surgery on remission and recurrence rates of pituitary adenomas: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

Notable
Tzu chi medical journal · 2026

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 42 studies (6,416 patients, 2010–2025) found that endoscopic TSS (ETSS) achieved significantly higher endocrine remission rates than microscopic TSS (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.12–1.40), with descriptively lower recurrence rates (7.1% vs. 11.8%), though no direct comparative studies addressed recurrence as a primary endpoint. Gross total resection rates did not differ significantly between approaches. Visual remission following ETSS was reported in 81.8% of patients with optic chiasm compression, supporting its role as the preferred surgical modality for most PitNETs.

Caveat: Recurrence comparisons are descriptive only—no direct head-to-head RCTs were available for this outcome. Significant heterogeneity exists across included studies in tumor types, surgeon experience, and remission criteria; the predominantly retrospective cohort design limits causal inference.
PMID 42746593 · doi.org/10.4103/tcmj.TCMJ-D-25-00145
Case: A 45-year-old man with Cushing's disease is being counseled on surgical options. His neurosurgeon is experienced in both microscopic and endoscopic transsphenoidal approaches. Based on current meta-analytic evidence, what advantage does the endoscopic approach confer regarding endocrine remission?
The meta-analysis demonstrated a significantly higher endocrine remission rate with ETSS versus microscopic TSS (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.12–1.40), with descriptively lower recurrence rates (7.1% vs. 11.8%). For a patient with functioning PitNET such as Cushing's disease, endoscopic approach appears to offer a meaningful advantage in achieving hormonal remission, making it the preferred approach when expertise is available.

Prescription pattern of oral hydrocortisone by Indian healthcare professionals: An online questionnaire-based survey.

Preliminary
Endocrine regulations · 2026

This survey of 300 Indian healthcare professionals (84% response rate) found that oral hydrocortisone is widely and frequently prescribed for primary and secondary adrenal insufficiency and CAH, with high awareness of approved indications and tapering protocols. However, hyperglycemia and weight gain were the most frequently reported adverse effects (each ~43%), and nearly 90% of respondents expressed a desire for sustained-release hydrocortisone formulations—particularly for primary adrenal insufficiency—reflecting a recognized unmet clinical need that mirrors findings from Western practice surveys. Prednisolone was the most common alternative when hydrocortisone was not used.

Caveat: Online survey methodology is subject to selection bias toward more engaged or specialist clinicians; the sample may not be representative of all Indian prescribers. Responses are self-reported and not validated against actual prescribing records.
PMID 42762503 · doi.org/10.2478/enr-2026-0022
Case: A patient with primary adrenal insufficiency on conventional thrice-daily oral hydrocortisone reports persistent fatigue, particularly in the morning, and her HbA1c has risen. She asks whether there is a better formulation. What does current evidence and clinician survey data suggest about the unmet need here?
Conventional immediate-release hydrocortisone has an unphysiological pharmacokinetic profile and is associated with metabolic adverse effects including hyperglycemia and weight gain. Survey data confirm that ~90% of clinicians recognize the need for modified-release formulations. Once-daily modified-release hydrocortisone (e.g., Plenadren) better mimics the physiological cortisol rhythm and has been associated with improved metabolic profiles in clinical trials, making it a clinically reasonable option to discuss in this patient.
