Progression of bilateral adrenal incidentalomas: a monocentric cohort study.

Notable
Frontiers in endocrinology · 2026

In this retrospective single-center cohort of 120 patients with bilateral adrenal incidentalomas (BAIs) followed for at least 36 months, 32% of initially non-functioning lesions developed mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS) over time, and 44% showed radiological progression. Notably, radiological and hormonal progression were largely independent—only 7.4% of patients with radiological progression also developed biochemical progression. Higher baseline 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test (DST) values predicted both biochemical progression and more substantial radiological growth (>10 mm). These findings highlight that current ESE/ENS@T guidelines, which lack specific BAI-tailored recommendations, may be insufficient for this subgroup.

Caveat: Single-center retrospective design with modest sample size (120 enrolled, 61-64 completing full follow-up) limits generalizability; prospective multicenter studies are needed to define optimal surveillance intervals for BAIs specifically.
PMID 42755597 · doi.org/10.3389/fendo.2026.1912889
Case: A 62-year-old woman with bilateral adrenal incidentalomas has a baseline 1-mg DST cortisol of 1.35 µg/dL (non-functioning by current criteria) and no radiological features of malignancy. Based on this cohort study, what is the approximate risk that she will develop MACS over a 3-year follow-up, and what baseline parameter best predicts this transition?
Approximately 32% of initially non-functioning BAI patients developed MACS during follow-up. Higher baseline 1-mg DST cortisol values (even within the 'non-functioning' range) were the only parameter that significantly predicted biochemical progression, underscoring the importance of retesting cortisol autonomy over time in BAI patients.

MRI-based radiomics prediction of somatostatin receptor ligand response in acromegaly: an automated pipeline study.

Preliminary
Journal of the Endocrine Society · 2026

This multicenter study developed a fully automated MRI-based radiomics pipeline—incorporating tumor detection (YOLOv8), 3D segmentation, and machine-learning classification—to predict somatostatin receptor ligand (SRL) response in 49 acromegaly patients. The best-performing model, a 3-feature logistic regression using contrast-enhanced T1-weighted features, achieved an out-of-fold AUC of 0.798 and correctly classified 79.6% of patients. The most consistently discriminative feature was a gray-level co-occurrence matrix texture feature (ClusterShade), appearing in 98% of cross-validation folds. This represents a promising step toward non-invasive, pre-treatment prediction of SRL response to guide therapy selection.

Caveat: The treatment-response cohort is small (n=49), and the pipeline was not externally validated in an independent acromegaly-specific dataset; results should be considered hypothesis-generating until prospective validation in larger multicenter cohorts is completed.
PMID 42751035 · doi.org/10.1210/jendso/bvag198
Case: A 38-year-old man with newly diagnosed acromegaly secondary to a macroadenoma is being considered for primary medical therapy with a somatostatin receptor ligand versus upfront surgery. According to this radiomics study, what imaging modality and analytical approach showed the best performance for predicting SRL response, and what was the reported AUC?
Contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI features, analyzed via an automated radiomics pipeline with a 3-feature logistic regression classifier, achieved the best discriminative performance with an out-of-fold AUC of 0.798 (95% CI: 0.671–0.918), correctly classifying ~80% of patients as SRL responders or non-responders.

Osteoporosis and Skeletal Fragility in non-Metastatic Pheochromocytomas and Paragangliomas: The Adrenergic-Skeletal Axis.

Notable
Calcified tissue international · 2026

This review synthesizes emerging evidence that chronic catecholamine excess in pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma (PPGL) causes clinically significant skeletal harm—including increased bone turnover, reduced bone mineral density, impaired trabecular quality, and higher vertebral fracture prevalence—mediated through sympathetic innervation of bone and adrenergic receptor signaling on osteoblasts and osteoclasts. The review also raises the hypothesis that specific genetic backgrounds (VHL and SDHx mutations) may further modulate skeletal phenotype, independent of catecholamine secretion. The authors advocate for routine bone health screening in PPGL patients, a practice not yet standard in most centers.

Caveat: The available clinical dataset is limited to a small number of studies with heterogeneous designs; the proposed role of VHL/SDHx mutations as skeletal phenotype modulators is described as speculative and requires prospective validation. This is a narrative review and does not provide new primary data.
PMID 42758342 · doi.org/10.1007/s00223-026-01607-6
Case: A 45-year-old woman with a biochemically active, non-metastatic pheochromocytoma is referred for preoperative evaluation. She has no known risk factors for osteoporosis and is premenopausal. Should bone health assessment be part of her workup, and what is the pathophysiological rationale according to the adrenergic-skeletal axis concept?
Yes—emerging evidence supports bone health screening in PPGL patients regardless of classic osteoporosis risk factors. Catecholamine excess activates β-adrenergic receptors on osteoblasts (suppressing bone formation) and promotes osteoclast activity, resulting in increased bone turnover and reduced BMD. Clinical studies have documented higher vertebral fracture prevalence in PPGL patients. DXA and vertebral fracture assessment should be considered, particularly in patients with long-standing or high catecholamine burden.
