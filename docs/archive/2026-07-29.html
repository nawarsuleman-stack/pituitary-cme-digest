Stereotactic Radiosurgery for Cushing's Disease: An International Multicenter Study.

Practice-changing
European journal of endocrinology · 2026

This large international multicenter retrospective study (n=361, 19 centers, median follow-up 52 months) found that single-session Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKRS) achieved durable off-medication biochemical remission in 59% of Cushing's disease patients at 60 months, with 95% local tumor control. Importantly, a shorter interval between transsphenoidal surgery (TSS) failure and SRS referral was a significant predictor of remission, while pre-SRS medical therapy, higher morning cortisol, and whole-sella irradiation each predicted lower remission rates. New endocrinopathy developed in 32% of patients, most commonly hypocortisolism (19%) and central hypothyroidism (13%), underscoring the need for long-term surveillance. These findings support prompt SRS referral after TSS failure and argue against whole-sella treatment when target delineation is feasible.

Caveat: Retrospective multicenter design with inherent selection and referral biases; heterogeneity across 19 centers in patient selection, pre-SRS medication use, and follow-up protocols may confound outcome estimates. Recurrence in 11.6% at a median of 37 months means long-term remission durability beyond 5 years requires further prospective study.
PMID 43070125 · doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag231
Case: A 42-year-old woman with confirmed Cushing's disease undergoes transsphenoidal surgery but has persistent hypercortisolemia at 3-month follow-up. She is started on ketoconazole while awaiting further treatment. Based on this multicenter SRS study, what factors favor deferring SRS in this scenario, and what does the data suggest about timing of SRS referral?
Pre-SRS medical therapy was associated with lower remission rates in this study, and a shorter TSS-to-SRS interval predicted greater remission. This suggests that initiating bridging medical therapy prior to SRS may reduce the likelihood of biochemical remission from radiosurgery. The data support prompt SRS referral after TSS failure rather than prolonged medical management before proceeding, though bridging may be necessary for severe hypercortisolism. Whole-sella irradiation should be avoided if the target can be discretely delineated, as it was associated with both lower remission and greater need for salvage treatment.

2026 Consensus on the Management of Cushing's Disease: A Collaborative Statement from the Korean Endocrine Society and Japan Endocrine Society.

Notable
Endocrinology and metabolism (Seoul, Korea) · 2026

The Korean Endocrine Society and Japan Endocrine Society jointly issued a 2026 expert consensus on the management of Cushing's disease, applicable to East Asian practice contexts. Transsphenoidal surgery remains first-line when feasible; individualized subsequent management considers disease/tumor characteristics, severity of hypercortisolism, comorbidities, and regional medication availability. Medical therapy — including pituitary-directed agents and adrenal steroidogenesis inhibitors — is recommended for persistent/recurrent disease, as a bridge pre-surgery, or when surgery is contraindicated; available agents and reimbursement differ between Korea and Japan. Bilateral adrenalectomy is reserved for refractory cases, and radiation therapy is considered when surgery and medical therapy fail.

Caveat: This is a consensus/expert opinion document, not a systematic review or meta-analysis; evidence gaps are explicitly acknowledged. Recommendations are tailored to East Asian healthcare and regulatory environments and may not translate directly to other practice settings. The same statement appears to have been simultaneously published in two journals (PMID 43072764 in Endocrine Journal), suggesting coordinated dual publication rather than independent new evidence.

Case: A 55-year-old man in South Korea has biopsy-confirmed Cushing's disease with a 6 mm ACTH-secreting adenoma. He undergoes transsphenoidal surgery but has persistent elevated UFC at 3 months postoperatively. His comorbidities include poorly controlled hypertension and new-onset diabetes. According to the 2026 KES-JES consensus, what is the recommended next step, and what factors should guide selection of medical therapy?
Per the 2026 KES-JES consensus, medical therapy is recommended for persistent disease after surgery. Selection should be individualized based on disease severity, comorbidities, and regional medication availability and reimbursement status. Both pituitary tumor-directed agents (e.g., pasireotide where available) and adrenal steroidogenesis inhibitors (e.g., metyrapone, osilodrostat) are key pharmacologic options. Radiation therapy can be considered if medical therapy is insufficient, and bilateral adrenalectomy is reserved for refractory, highly selected cases. The specific agent chosen will depend on what is locally approved and reimbursed.

Investigating the Lived Experience of Loved Ones Living With Persons With Adrenal Insufficiency.

Notable
Clinical medicine insights. Endocrinology and diabetes · 2026

This qualitative study — the first to specifically examine loved ones (LOs) of people with adrenal insufficiency (AI) — conducted 15 semi-structured interviews and identified five key themes: imposition of caregiving roles, filling care gaps without formal training, constant fear of adrenal crisis, pervasive day-to-day disease impact, and magnitude of LO burden being tied to perceived patient independence. LOs frequently performed clinical monitoring and sick-day rule implementation without having received structured education, paralleling well-documented caregiver burden patterns in diabetes. The study identifies significant unmet needs for LO education and support as targets for future intervention. These findings have direct implications for how endocrinologists counsel and resource the household unit of AI patients, not just the patients themselves.

Caveat: Small qualitative sample (n=15) recruited from a single academic endocrinology clinic, limiting generalizability. Criterion sampling may have selected for more engaged caregivers. Findings are hypothesis-generating and descriptive rather than confirmatory; quantitative validation of burden metrics is needed.
PMID 43069892 · doi.org/10.1177/11795514261505274
Case: You are following a 38-year-old woman with primary adrenal insufficiency on hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone. Her husband accompanies her and mentions he is frequently waking at night to check on her and feels anxious about what to do if she becomes unwell. Based on this qualitative study, what does the evidence suggest about the unmet needs of partners of AI patients, and what practical action should this prompt at today's visit?
This study found that loved ones of AI patients routinely fill care gaps — including crisis recognition and sick-day management — without formal training, and live with persistent fear of adrenal crisis. This should prompt the clinician to directly address the husband's education needs: providing structured sick-day rule instruction, emergency hydrocortisone injection training, and clear crisis action plans directed at him as well as the patient. The study suggests that targeting LO education and support, not just the patient, may improve outcomes and reduce caregiver burden.
